# 2. Abstract

The recruitment industry is undergoing a structural shift driven by Artificial Intelligence.
Traditional hiring pipelines rely on recruiters manually reading resumes, a process that does not
scale as applicant volumes grow and that is susceptible to inconsistency and unconscious bias. This
project investigates the business opportunity and industry trend of AI-driven recruitment technology
through secondary research: industry survey data, vendor and standards documentation, and trade
commentary on HR-tech.

The study traces the limitations of traditional Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS), which rely on
keyword matching and frequently overlook qualified candidates whose resumes are worded differently
from a job description. It then examines how large language models (LLMs) enable a different
approach — reading a resume the way a human recruiter would and inferring skills, experience, and
seniority — illustrated through **SkillMatch AI**, a recruitment-platform concept that combines a
candidate/recruiter web application with an AI engine (OpenAI GPT-4o-mini) that scores each resume
against open job postings using a weighted, multi-factor model (required skills, experience level,
semantic fit, and nice-to-have skills).

Drawing on current industry adoption data (SHRM, 2025; Gartner, 2025), the report evaluates this
approach against secondary sources on HR-tech best practice, including the operational cost of AI at
scale and the well-documented risk of algorithmic bias in automated candidate screening. The report
concludes that AI-assisted screening is a viable and increasingly standard approach for recruitment
platforms, provided it is paired with a transparent scoring methodology, human oversight, and active
safeguards against bias.

**Keywords:** Artificial Intelligence, recruitment technology, resume screening, candidate–job
matching, HR-tech, applicant tracking systems, algorithmic bias.
