# 3. Introduction of the Study

## 3.1 Topic and Industry Area

This project studies a recent and fast-growing business trend: the application of Artificial
Intelligence to online recruitment, commonly referred to as **HR-tech** or **AI recruitment
technology**. The area of study sits at the intersection of two industries — Human Resources
(talent acquisition) and Software/AI Technology — and is chosen because it reflects a real,
observable shift in how companies hire, driven by the availability of large language models (LLMs)
such as OpenAI's GPT family.

## 3.2 Background

As companies increasingly hire through online job boards and career portals, the number of
applications per posting has grown far beyond what a recruiter can manually review. Traditional
Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) largely rely on keyword matching — a method that misses qualified
candidates whose resumes are worded differently from the job description, and that offers recruiters
no genuine understanding of a candidate's overall fit. The emergence of generative AI models capable
of reading unstructured text and producing structured understanding (skills, experience, seniority)
has created an opportunity to replace or augment keyword-based ATS filtering with semantic matching.
Industry data supports this as an active, not theoretical, shift: recent surveys report that a
majority of HR professionals already use AI somewhere in their recruiting workflow (Society for Human
Resource Management, 2025a).

## 3.3 Illustrative Example — SkillMatch AI

To ground this secondary research in a concrete example, the report refers throughout to
**SkillMatch AI**, a recruitment-platform concept designed to explore this trend. At a high level, it
connects two user groups — job seekers, who build profiles and upload resumes, and recruiters, who
post job listings and review applicants — and uses an AI engine to parse uploaded resumes and produce
a weighted match score against open job postings, so recruiters see a ranked shortlist rather than raw
resumes. The example is used only to make the discussion of matching methodology, cost governance, and
bias risk concrete; the report's conclusions rest on the secondary literature, not on this example
alone.

## 3.4 Scope of the Study

This report focuses on: (a) the industry trend of AI adoption in recruitment, (b) the general
methodology by which AI-based platforms typically score and rank candidates, and (c) an evaluation of
that methodology against secondary sources on HR-tech best practice — transparency, cost governance,
and bias risk. The report does not involve primary data collection such as user surveys, interviews,
or live user analytics; all analysis is based on secondary literature and documentation review.

## 3.5 Application of Prior Learning

This study draws on prior coursework in business and technology management — market/industry
analysis, business-opportunity evaluation, and information-systems design — and applies it to a live
industry trend rather than treating AI adoption in recruitment as a purely technical question. This
is most visible in Sections 6–8, where methodology, cost-governance, and access-control analysis are
each applications of core-program subject matter to this project's chosen topic.
