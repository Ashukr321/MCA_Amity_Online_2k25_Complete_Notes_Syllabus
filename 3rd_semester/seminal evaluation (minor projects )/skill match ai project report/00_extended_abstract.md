# Extended Abstract / Project Summary

**Title:** AI-Powered Resume Screening and Candidate–Job Matching in Recruitment

Online recruitment has grown to a scale where the volume of applications received per job posting
has made manual resume screening slow, inconsistent, and prone to human bias. This project studies
the emerging industry trend of applying Artificial Intelligence (AI) to recruitment — commonly known
as HR-tech — and examines how AI can be used to automate resume screening and candidate–job matching
in place of traditional keyword-based filtering.

The study is based entirely on secondary research — industry survey data, technology-vendor
documentation, and trade commentary on AI-driven HR technology — supplemented by a brief illustrative
example, **SkillMatch AI**, a conceptual recruitment-platform design that connects job seekers and
recruiters and uses a large language model (GPT-4o-mini) to extract skills and experience from
resumes and compute a weighted match score against job postings.

The report first traces the shift from keyword-based Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) to
semantic, AI-based matching, citing current adoption figures (SHRM, 2025; Gartner, 2025) showing that
a majority of HR professionals already use AI in some part of the recruiting workflow. It then
analyzes how a weighted, multi-factor scoring approach — balancing required skills, experience, and
contextual/semantic fit — produces more transparent and defensible rankings than a single opaque
"match percentage." It further discusses two recurring themes in the literature: the operational cost
of running AI at scale, and the risk of algorithmic bias in AI-driven candidate screening, drawing on
documented cases where AI hiring tools have disadvantaged qualified candidates.

The report concludes with recommendations for organizations adopting AI in recruitment — favoring
transparent, explainable scoring, human oversight, and periodic bias review — and situates AI-assisted
screening as a practical and increasingly standard response to a genuine scaling problem in the
recruitment industry.
