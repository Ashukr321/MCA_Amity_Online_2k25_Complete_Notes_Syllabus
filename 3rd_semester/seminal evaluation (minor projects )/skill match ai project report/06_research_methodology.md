# 6. Research Methodology

## 6.1 Research Type

This is a **secondary (desk) research** project, in accordance with the guideline's requirement that
no primary data collection be undertaken. No surveys, interviews, or user experiments were conducted.

## 6.2 Data Sources

Secondary sources used for this study include:

- Industry survey data on AI adoption in recruiting (SHRM Talent Trends research, Gartner
  talent-acquisition trend reports).
- Trade commentary and legal analysis on algorithmic hiring bias.
- General technical documentation on the technologies typically used to build such platforms
  (large language model APIs, web application frameworks, cloud database and file-storage services).
- A brief illustrative example (SkillMatch AI), used only to make abstract discussion of
  architecture and scoring methodology concrete.

## 6.3 Method of Analysis

The study followed a **literature review and comparative analysis** method:

1. **Collection** — relevant secondary sources on AI-in-recruitment adoption, matching methodology,
   cost governance, and bias risk were gathered from industry research bodies and trade publications.
2. **Comparison** — the general architecture and scoring approach used by AI recruitment platforms
   were compared against this literature to identify what constitutes established best practice.
3. **Interpretation** — findings were interpreted against the study's objectives: does AI-based
   matching solve the screening-volume problem, is a weighted scoring methodology defensible, and
   what safeguards (security, cost, bias) does the literature recommend for responsible adoption.

## 6.4 Limitations

- The analysis is based on published industry survey data and secondary literature current as of the
  time of writing; it does not include primary interviews, live production metrics from a specific
  deployed system, or controlled hiring outcome studies.
- No formal academic citation index was queried for this report; sources are drawn from established
  industry research bodies (SHRM, Gartner) and trade/legal commentary, consistent with the guideline's
  allowance for "internet research... trade magazines, news sites."
